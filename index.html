03Why

My name is Blake. I'm 26 and live in Raleigh, North Carolina. Until a year ago I had never pushed past a real limit in my life.

That changed in the fall of 2025, when I started training. Training for a half marathon in Wilmington this past February and a full marathon in Nashville in April. I trained seriously, beat my goal time, and collected the compliments, praise, and people saying that they could never. It felt great, but underneath something was missing: I couldn't answer the simplest question. Why are you doing this? A medal wasn't a good enough reason. "To see if I could" didn't do it. I knew I couldn't stop at a marathon, and I knew the next distance wouldn't be the answer either.

This July I ran 40 miles over eight hours through the Carolina heat. Somewhere deep in that run, legs gone, the voice in my head making its case for quitting, I found what I never expected to find there: immense gratitude and a heart warmed by God to take action. Over the past year I've learned that the desire to quit can be overcome by recognizing my suffering for what it actually is: a privilege.

We get to choose to accomplish hard things. We schedule it, we train for it, and when it's over the suffering stops. Some people wake up inside a challenge they never chose and can't stop. No training block, no aid stations, no cutoff time. The only common ground between that and an endurance challenge is the fear of not making it to the finish.

Honestly, I've been running for barely a year. I have no idea if I can run 100 miles or if this movement will raise a single dollar. I'm pledging to put the same effort into both anyway. Every training mile logged in public and every dollar routed straight to the cause. I'm letting you watch whether it works or not.

We get to quit. Some people don't get that option. That's why I won't.

What scares me isn't mile 70 at three in the morning with 30 miles to go. It's living a comfortable life without suffering, never finding meaning. Both Clocks is how I make sure that doesn't happen.

If reading this stirs something, trade it for motivation. If my suffering moves you, I encourage you to take a step towards sharing this campaign with those around you. Or just go do your own hard thing.

04Causes
The model

One challenge. One cause. Every dollar to them.

Each challenge sponsors one charity announced before the event, and every donation goes straight to that charity through its own donation page or through the proceeds of Both Clocks Merchandise.

01

A challenge gets chosen

Something long, hard, and worth watching. Honestly, whatever earns the most attention. First up is 100 miles at the Southern Tour Ultra.

02

A cause gets sponsored

One charity per event, serving people in the middle of something they never chose. Announced before the event, with the reasons why published right here.

How I pick a cause

Four criteria, checked in this order:

Serving the unchosen. Cancer, hunger, abuse, neglect, disease, loss — the cause serves people living through something nobody signs up for.

Small enough that the money moves the needle. A five-figure gift to a national organization disappears. To a local one, it's a visible fraction of a year.

Direct service, not regranting. The charity does the work itself. There's no layer between the donors and the person helped.

A clean unit of impact. You should be able to say exactly what a gift bought, which matters when you're asking people for money for months on end.

The first sponsored cause — for the Southern Tour Ultra 100 — will be announced here before race day.
